The second fall of rock took the lamp hook off Ivo's belt and most of the skin off his left shoulder. He lay on his side in the dust with his mouth shut and counted. Four seconds, and nothing more came down. He counted to four again to be sure, then felt for the hook in the dark.

The gallery behind him was gone. Ahead, the old working ran twenty feet before the timbers gave way to raw slate, and past the slate the air was moving. He could feel it on the wet side of his face, cold and sour, the smell the tithe vault gave off on the days the warden opened it.

He pushed stonesense into the rock the way Hale had taught him, palm flat, and let it spread. The slate answered in layers: loose shale above, hard stone below, and between them a gap no wider than his forearm that ran uphill and to the right.

A gap that size was for a boy of twelve. Ivo was nineteen and had a crew boss's shoulders from hauling rope.

He took off his jerkin and his shirt and went in head first.

The slate held him on both sides like a closing hand. He moved by the inch, toes pushing, fingers finding the next edge, and each time he emptied his lungs he slid a little further and each time he filled them the rock pressed back. Halfway, the seam narrowed and he stuck. He hung there with his arms stretched ahead of him and his ribs caught, and the sour air moved over his hands from the dark in front.

He emptied his lungs until his chest ached and pulled. Skin went. The seam let him go.

His hands came out into open air and touched something smooth, square and warm, then another beside it. He dragged himself out onto a floor of stacked tithe slates, hundreds of them, and above them on a beam a lantern that someone had left burning low.
